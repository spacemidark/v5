# Assembly, shrouds & seals

> **Key concept** · A vane row becomes an operable stator assembly only when interfaces and load paths are controlled.

A fixed stator can be implemented using individually retained vanes, circumferential segments, inner/outer shroud assemblies or other engine-specific architectures. Supports may change radial stiffness, vibratory modes and thermal constraints.

Shrouds and casing interfaces affect sealing, alignment and local leakage. Inner-hub treatments can include seals or honeycomb features in some designs; do not assume every stator has identical hardware. Bushings are strongly associated with pivoting VSV systems but are not mandatory parts of every fixed stator row.

Assembly analysis includes relative motion, growth, contact stress, anti-rotation features, retention under abnormal loads, maintainability and tolerance stack-up.

<EngineeringNote>
Treat airfoil, platform, shroud, seal and casing as a linked load-and-clearance system.
</EngineeringNote>
