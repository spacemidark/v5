# Loads & structural design

> **Key concept** · Stator vanes must carry aerodynamic pressure, dynamic excitation and thermal/interface loads safely into the support structure.

The pressure distribution on the airfoil generates resultant forces and moments. Depending on the support concept, loads pass through platforms, roots, segment rails, shrouds and casing features. A cantilever vane differs materially from a vane restrained at both ends.

Mechanical assessment may include static stress, local stress concentrations, contact loads, fretting, interface slip, tolerance stack-up and transient conditions. Airfoils in a fixed stator are not rotor blades: high steady centrifugal loading from rotating with the shaft does not apply to the vane itself.

A first sizing pass may treat the vane as a beam, but pressure distribution, twist, real stiffness, root fillets and support conditions require a properly constrained 3D structural model before design closure.

<EngineeringNote>
The “critical section” is frequently at an interface, fillet or contact feature—not necessarily at midspan.
</EngineeringNote>
