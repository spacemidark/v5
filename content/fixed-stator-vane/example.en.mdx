# Worked engineering examples

> **Key concept** · Simple calculations create intuition; they are not a substitute for stage-specific design data or validated simulation.

Example A — thermal growth: for a free 80 mm segment with α = 15 × 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹ and uniform ΔT = 250 K, the estimated ΔL is 0.30 mm. Constrained expansion can instead develop stress.

Example B — blade passing: for an illustrative 42-blade upstream rotor at 9,000 RPM, the first blade-passing frequency is 42 × 9,000 / 60 = 6,300 Hz. Actual modal excitation depends on harmonics, amplitude, nodal patterns and support conditions.

Example C — geometric parameter: for airfoil chord c = 30 mm and pitch s = 20 mm, solidity σ = 1.5. This is a geometry ratio, not a statement of aerodynamic acceptability.

<EngineeringNote>
All numbers above are fictitious teaching inputs; they are not taken from or representative of a particular engine.
</EngineeringNote>

## Example equation

$$\Delta L = \alpha \cdot L_0 \cdot \Delta T$$

This expression represents idealized free, one-dimensional thermal expansion.
