# Overview & role

> **Key concept** · A fixed stator vane is a stationary aerodynamic element positioned between rotating compressor blade rows. It changes the direction and distribution of airflow to prepare the inlet conditions for the next rotor.

An axial compressor is organized into successive blade rows. Rotors exchange shaft work with the air; fixed stators do not rotate and do not directly supply shaft work. Many compressor stators act as diffusers, reducing absolute velocity while increasing static pressure, subject to the local flow design.

A stator is a full assembly, not just an airfoil. Its functional boundary can include inner/outer platforms, shrouds, vane retention features, seals, casing interfaces and supporting hardware. Details vary substantially between engines and stages.

<EngineeringNote>
Distinguish a fixed stator from a VSV: a VSV can rotate about a trunnion to schedule vane angle; a fixed stator has no commanded variable incidence.
</EngineeringNote>
