# Vibration & fatigue

> **Key concept** · Rotating wakes and unsteady pressure fields can excite stationary vanes even though the vanes themselves do not rotate.

Candidate excitations include rotor blade passing, harmonics, rotating-stall patterns and broadband turbulent forcing. For rotor blade count Nᵣ and shaft speed in revolutions per minute, blade-passing frequency is f_BPF = Nᵣ × RPM / 60. This is one excitation family, not a full forcing model.

Natural frequencies and modes depend on vane geometry, support conditions, contacts, material stiffness, temperature and added structural features. A Campbell diagram helps screen crossings; forced-response and high-cycle fatigue assessments are needed where relevant.

In-service damage, fillet details and fretting can sharply reduce fatigue margin. Verification can combine validated finite element analysis with instrumentation, modal tests and appropriate endurance evidence.

<EngineeringNote>
A frequency crossing is a screening flag; by itself it does not prove unacceptable stress—or safe operation.
</EngineeringNote>
